Introduction

Background

UPI has become the default way to pay in India. In FY2025-26 it handled about 24,162 crore transactions worth ₹314.23 lakh crore upi_fy26, upi_fy26b, and by June 2026 around 55.49 crore people had been onboarded upi_users. NPCI publishes these numbers every month npci_stats. Paying a vegetable seller, a hostel mess or an electricity bill now takes a few seconds.

The same speed is what makes UPI useful to fraudsters. Government replies in Parliament put UPI fraud at about 13.42 lakh cases (₹1,087 crore) in FY2023-24 and 12.64 lakh cases (₹981 crore) in FY2024-25 upi_fraud_ls. Across all channels, reported cyber financial fraud losses were ₹22,845.73 crore in 2024, up from ₹7,465.18 crore in 2023 i4c_loss.

What surprised us when we started reading about this is that most of these cases are not hacks. The victim opens their own app, types the amount, enters their own PIN and sends the money, because someone on the phone has convinced them it is a KYC fee, a refund, a police deposit or a job registration charge. This is usually called authorised push payment (APP) fraud. PINs and OTPs cannot stop it, because the right person is entering them.

Motivation

Messages like "your KYC will expire today" or "your electricity will be disconnected tonight" are something almost every UPI user in India has seen, and surveys show that many people actually lose money to them: in a 2025 LocalCircles survey, about one in five UPI-using families said they had faced UPI fraud since 2022 localcircles. Once the money is sent it moves through mule accounts quickly, and getting it back is slow. The government's fraud reporting system has saved more than ₹11,158 crore by June 2026 cfcfrms_saved, which is a lot, but it is still small compared to what is lost.

So we asked a simple question: what if the app checked the payment before the money left, and warned the person in plain words when something looked wrong? The platform already knows things the payer does not, for example that the receiving account was created two days ago, or that fifteen strangers paid it today. Fraud detection research has used this kind of behavioural signal for a long time bolton2002, dalpozzolo2014. The part we felt was missing was the payer side: telling the person why, at the moment they are about to pay.

The project also covered most of what we have studied: machine learning on very imbalanced data, backend design where a few hundred milliseconds matter, database consistency for money, and UI design that people actually pay attention to.

Objectives

The objectives we set at the start were:

  1. Score every payment for scam risk before it is authorised, and return a 0 to 100 score, a band (LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH) and the reasons in plain language, within 300 ms at the 95th percentile.
  2. Combine simple rules with a calibrated ML model, and check on a fair time-based test whether this hybrid catches more scams than rules alone at the same false alarm rate. We agreed to report the result even if the hybrid lost.
  3. Respond in proportion to risk: nothing for LOW, a warning with a few questions for MEDIUM, and a forced pause with re-typing of the UPI ID for HIGH.
  4. Give fraud analysts a live alert queue, and let their decisions block confirmed scam accounts for every user.
  5. Keep payments working even when the ML service is slow or down.

Scope

We built a working UPI-style wallet with fake money, the full risk pipeline, the payer screens, an analyst console and an admin console, plus a synthetic data generator and an evaluation setup. We did not connect to any real bank or to NPCI, we did not handle card or net banking fraud, and we could not test on real fraud data, since students do not get access to it. All users, payments and scams in this report are synthetic.
