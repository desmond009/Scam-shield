User Research

We wanted the design decisions (which signals to use, how much friction is acceptable, how to word a warning) to come from evidence and not only from our own guesses. We used three sources: published government data and large surveys, especially the 2025 LocalCircles survey of over 32,000 respondents from 365 districts localcircles; our own questionnaire (Appendix A); and research on how people react to security warnings akhawe2013, anderson2015, vance2018.

Questionnaire

We prepared a 20-question online questionnaire that takes under five minutes on a phone. It is given in full in Appendix A. It has five sections: about the respondent and how often they use UPI (Q1 to Q5); whether they have faced scam attempts and what kind (Q6 to Q10); losses and whether money was recovered or reported (Q11 to Q13); how they feel about warnings, a 10-second pause and three quick questions before a risky payment (Q14 to Q18); and whether they are comfortable with payment patterns being analysed for fraud prevention (Q19 to Q20). Our hypotheses were that most people have faced scam attempts, that victims rarely get money back or report it, and that people would accept a short delay on a risky payment if it is rare and explained.

Responses and Key Insights

The published surveys already support the first two hypotheses. About 20 % of UPI-using families in the LocalCircles survey had faced fraud since 2022, around 40 % of victims lost money after clicking a payment link localcircles, and about 51 % never complained localcircles_nc. "Digital arrest" cases, which are purely social engineering, went from 39,925 in 2022 to 1,23,672 in 2024 digital_arrest.

From the reading and the personas, these are the points that most changed our design:

  • Since the real owner enters the PIN, we score the payment itself and not just the login, and we ask three questions about things the data cannot see, like whether someone is on the phone pushing them to pay.
  • People ignore warnings they see often, so LOW payments show nothing at all, and warnings name specific facts ("this account is 2 days old") with at most three reasons.
  • Scammers rely on hurry, so a HIGH payment gets a 10-second pause and the UPI ID must be typed again. "Cancel and report" is the biggest button.
  • A victim being coached on the phone may lie, so the answers to the questions can only increase the risk score, never reduce it.
  • Shopkeepers and festival shoppers behave unusually but honestly, so each user is compared with their own 30-day history, and our training data includes these cases.
  • Analysts like Priya need reasons, the ML score and the payee's history together, and one click to block a confirmed scam account for everyone.
