Market and Competitive Analysis

Market Size

We see ScamShield as a B2B product. The customers would be institutions that send UPI payments for their users, like cooperative banks, small finance banks and smaller payment apps (TPAPs), and the warning screens would be given to them as an SDK. End users would not pay anything.

For the total market we took published estimates of the Indian fraud detection and prevention market. These vary a lot: MarketsandMarkets puts it at about US$ 990 million in 2026, growing 22.4 % a year mkt_mm, while IMARC says US$ 1.4 billion in 2024, growing about 20 % a year mkt_imarc. We used the lower one. For the serviceable market we worked from UPI volume: about 241.6 billion transactions in FY2025-26 upi_fy26. If each payment check cost 2 paise (our own assumption), checking every UPI payment would be worth about ₹483 crore a year. For what we could realistically get in five years, we assumed 1 % of UPI volume from 5 to 10 smaller banks and apps.

Existing Solutions

We grouped the existing solutions by where they act: inside the bank, on the receiving account, on the phone number, after the fraud, or in the payer's app. This matters for us because ScamShield acts at one specific point, in the payer's app just before the money leaves. For each one we only describe what is publicly documented, and we mark anything we could not confirm.

Bank fraud risk management (FRM) platforms. Commercial products such as FICO Falcon, Clari5, SAS and Feedzai are sold to banks for monitoring transactions. We did not have access to their product documentation, so in Table 4.2 we only credit them with general capabilities that this kind of product is known for (real-time monitoring, case management). We mark payer-facing features as not public.

MuleHunter.AI. This is an RBI Innovation Hub model for spotting mule accounts. According to an RTI reply reported by MediaNama, about 23 banks were using it by December 2025 mulehunter, and I4C signed an agreement with RBIH on it in May 2026 mulehunter_mou. It looks at the receiving account and not at the payer's decision.

DoT Financial Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI). Launched in May 2025, it rates mobile numbers as medium, high or very high risk, and the report we cite says at least one payment app uses it to stop payments to very high risk numbers dot_fri. It gives one signal, the phone number.

Sanchar Saathi and Chakshu. These are DoT services where citizens report suspected fraud calls and messages, which has led to lakhs of connections being disconnected sancharsaathi. They work at the telecom level.

1930 helpline and CFCFRMS. Victims report fraud and the money still in the chain can be frozen, with more than ₹11,158 crore saved by June 2026 cfcfrms_saved. This happens after the payment.

Warnings inside UPI apps and caller ID apps. The large UPI apps show some warnings of their own, and caller ID apps flag spam calls. We could not find public technical descriptions of how either decides when to warn, so we have not made claims about their features.

International example. In the UK, since October 2024 banks have had to reimburse most APP fraud victims, with the cost shared between the sending and receiving banks uk_psr. We include it because it shows why banks elsewhere are investing in checks before payment.

SWOT Analysis

Strengths

  • Rules and calibrated ML together, with clear reasons
  • Warnings and friction aimed at the payer
  • Fast (p95 about 110 ms) and keeps working if ML is down
  • Evaluation that anyone can rerun

Weaknesses

  • Only synthetic data so far
  • No real bank or NPCI integration
  • The questions depend on the user being honest
  • Small student team with no sales experience

Opportunities

  • RBI's push for risk-based checks rbi_auth
  • Market growing about 20 % a year
  • Many small banks with no data science team
  • Public signals like DoT FRI to add later

Threats

  • Big apps and vendors adding similar features
  • Scammers coaching victims through warnings
  • Cost of compliance and audits
  • False alarms losing user trust

Unique Value Proposition

Our pitch in one line is: stop the scam before the money moves. ScamShield does not just produce a score for the bank. It tells the payer in one sentence why a payment looks wrong, asks the questions an analyst would ask, and only slows them down when the risk is real. When an analyst confirms a scam account, every other user is protected from it. And because it is light and runs on modest hardware, smaller banks and apps can afford it.
