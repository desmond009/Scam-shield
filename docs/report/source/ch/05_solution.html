Proposed Solution

Overview

ScamShield puts a risk check between "the user presses Pay" and "the money moves". A payment happens in two steps. The first step, check, works out the risk and never moves any money. The second step, confirm, moves the money, but only if the app sends back a short-lived token from the check and has completed whatever the risk band requires. Figure 5.1 shows the flow.

This addresses the root causes from Section 2.3 directly. The payment is scored on behaviour even when the PIN is correct. The payer is shown what the platform knows, such as the account's age and reports. The pause and re-typing break the hurry that scammers depend on. The three questions catch the phone call that the data cannot see. LOW payments get no warning at all, so warnings stay rare. And because reports and analyst verdicts apply to every user, one victim's report protects the next person.

Key Features

The prototype already has these features:

  • A hybrid risk engine with 20 features, 11 rules and a calibrated gradient boosting model, blended 40:60 into a score from 0 to 100.
  • Two hard overrides: payments to an analyst-confirmed scam account are blocked, and "someone asked me for an OTP or to install an app" always means HIGH.
  • Reasons in plain English for the user (top three) and full detail for analysts.
  • The LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH screens described above, with the three scam questions.
  • A 30-day baseline per user, and a reputation score per payee built from reports, account age and how many different people paid it in the last day.
  • An analyst console with live alerts, and an admin console with a rules editor, model evaluation, retraining, a scam simulator and a system health page.
  • A fallback to rules only if the ML service is slow or down, and payments that cannot be duplicated or double spent.

Later we would like to add graph-based detection of mule networks, external signals like DoT FRI, warnings in Hindi and Kannada, a "trusted contact" confirmation for senior citizens, and safer ways to roll out new models.

Business Model

We would charge institutions, not users. The main income would be a fee per checked payment (we assumed ₹0.02, dropping to ₹0.01 above 10 crore checks a month), plus a monthly subscription for the analyst and admin consoles (₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000 depending on seats), a one-time onboarding and tuning fee (₹5 to 15 lakh), and an on-premise licence for institutions that cannot use a hosted service. For the customer, the value is fewer fraud losses and support calls, less damage to their reputation, and something to show the regulator for risk-based checks.

Go-to-Market Strategy

We would start with a pilot at one cooperative or small finance bank, or a campus payment app. For the first few months ScamShield would run in shadow mode, scoring real payments without showing anything to users, so we can measure how it actually performs. Then we would switch on the warnings for a group of users who agree to it. With those results as a case study, the next step is other cooperative banks (through their federations and technology providers) and smaller payment apps, with a free tier up to some monthly volume. After that we would apply to RBI's regulatory sandbox and Innovation Hub programmes and try to partner with core banking and UPI switch vendors so ScamShield can be added as a plug-in.
