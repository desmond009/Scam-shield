Proposed Solution

Solution Overview

ScamShield inserts a risk check between “the user presses Pay” and “the money moves”. A payment is a two-phase operation: check, which scores the payment and never moves money, and confirm, which moves the money only if the client presents a valid, short-lived check token and has satisfied any intervention the risk band requires. Future features

  • Graph-based mule-ring detection over the payee–sender network; device fingerprinting.
  • Integration of external risk signals: DoT FRI for the payee's phone number, I4C Suspect Registry.
  • Multilingual reasons and voice prompts (the catalogue is already keyed by locale).
  • “Trusted contact” confirmation for senior citizens on HIGH-risk payments.
  • Model registry with shadow and canary deployment; drift monitoring.
  • Federated learning across institutions to share patterns without sharing customer data.

Business Model and Monetisation

ScamShield is offered as a B2B service to payment-originating institutions. The end user never pays.

The value to a customer is reduced fraud losses and support costs, protection from reputational damage, and evidence for regulators of risk-based controls. Where reimbursement liability exists (as in the UK since 2024 uk_psr), each prevented scam has a direct monetary value to the institution.

Go-to-Market Strategy

  1. Phase 1 — Pilot (months 0–6). Partner with one cooperative or small finance bank, or a university/campus payment app. Run ScamShield in shadow mode (scoring without intervening) to measure precision and recall on real data, then enable interventions for a consenting user cohort.
  2. Phase 2 — Early adopters (months 6–18). Use pilot results as a case study; target cooperative banks through their federations and technology service providers, and smaller TPAPs. Offer the risk-scoring API with a free tier up to a monthly volume.
  3. Phase 3 — Scale (months 18–36). Seek inclusion in sandbox programmes (RBI Regulatory Sandbox, RBI Innovation Hub); integrate with core banking and UPI switch vendors as a plug-in; add regional languages.

Channels: direct sales to bank risk teams, partnerships with core-banking and UPI-switch vendors, fintech conferences and hackathons, and publication of our evaluation methodology to build credibility. Positioning: “the explainable, payer-facing layer that complements your existing fraud monitoring.”
