Policies, Legal and Compliance

The prototype uses only synthetic data, so no law is engaged by the demonstration itself. A real deployment, however, would process personal and financial data of identifiable people and make decisions that affect their payments. This chapter sets out the policies and controls a launch would require. It reflects our reading of the relevant laws and regulations and is not legal advice; any launch would require review by qualified counsel and the partner institution's compliance team.

Privacy policy (summary of the proposed policy)

  1. What we collect. Account details (name, e-mail, UPI ID); payment details (amount, payee, time); a hashed device identifier; login and verification outcomes; answers to the scam-context questions; reports filed. We do not collect contacts, SMS, call logs, location, card numbers or bank credentials.
  2. Why. Solely to detect and prevent payment fraud and scams, to operate the wallet, and to meet legal obligations. Data is not used for advertising, credit scoring or sale to third parties.
  3. Legal basis. Consent obtained through a clear, itemised notice at onboarding (available in English and the user's language), and legitimate uses permitted by law where applicable.
  4. Retention. Raw behavioural features for 12 months (needed to retrain models and handle disputes); aggregated baselines for 30 days; audit logs and security logs as required by CERT-In and DPDP rules; deletion or anonymisation thereafter.
  5. Sharing. With the partner bank or PSP that holds the user's account; with law enforcement when legally required; confirmed-fraud payee identifiers may be shared with industry registries.
  6. User rights. Access a summary of data held, correction, erasure (subject to legal retention), grievance redressal within the statutory period, and nomination. Users can see every warning they received and every report they filed on the My Safety page.
  7. Security. Encryption in transit and at rest, access control, audit logging, breach-response plan (Section 7.3).
  8. Contact. A named Grievance Officer / Data Protection contact.

Terms of Service and User Consent

  • Nature of the service. ScamShield provides risk warnings; it lowers but cannot eliminate the risk of fraud, and does not guarantee that every scam will be caught or that every flagged payment is a scam.
  • User autonomy. For MEDIUM risk the user may continue after reading the warning. For HIGH risk the user may continue after the pause and verification. Only payments to analyst-confirmed scam accounts are blocked outright; such blocks can be appealed through the grievance channel.
  • Questionnaire. Answering is voluntary; answers are used only to assess the current payment and to improve detection.
  • Reports. Users must report in good faith; malicious reporting may lead to suspension. Reports alone never block a payee.
  • Consent flow. Consent is collected with an unbundled notice (separate from the wallet terms), can be withdrawn at any time, and withdrawal is as easy as giving consent. Since fraud screening is integral to the payment service, withdrawal may limit the ability to make payments through the partner app — this is disclosed up front.
  • Liability and disputes. Governed by the partner institution's customer-protection policy and applicable RBI directions; jurisdiction of Indian courts.

Accessibility and Ethical Considerations

Accessibility

ScamShield's users include senior citizens and first-time digital users. The interface targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA wcag, in line with the spirit of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 rpwd: risk is never conveyed by colour alone (each band has an icon and text label), the warning screens use short sentences, buttons are large and clearly labelled, and the layout works at 390 px width and in dark mode. Future work includes screen-reader testing, regional languages and voice read-out of HIGH warnings.

Ethics

Ethical risks and how the design responds
ConcernDesign response
False positives burden innocent usersThresholds chosen on measured false-positive rates (0.43 % at MEDIUM on the holdout); LOW payments see nothing; hard-negative scenarios in training; per-scenario false-alarm reporting (e.g. festival spending, merchant batches).
Bias against groups (e.g. seniors, new users)No demographic attributes are used as features; behaviour is compared to the user's own baseline. In production, false-positive rates should be monitored by user segment.
Paternalism vs autonomyFriction is proportional and explained; the user may proceed in all cases except confirmed scam accounts.
Dark patternsFriction is used only to slow down a likely scam, never to drive commercial outcomes; the safe option is prominent but every option is visible.
Automated decisionsEvery decision has human-readable reasons; analysts review MEDIUM/HIGH alerts; payee blocks require a human verdict.
Malicious reportingOne report per user per payee; reports raise risk but never block; analyst review is authoritative.
Honest communication of resultsWe report that results come from synthetic data, that ML-only ranks slightly better than hybrid, and the data leaks we found and fixed.
