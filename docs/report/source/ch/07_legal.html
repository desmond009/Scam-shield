Policies, Legal and Compliance

The prototype only uses synthetic data, so the demo itself does not raise legal issues. A real launch would process personal and financial data of real people and make decisions about their payments, so this chapter lists what that would need. This is our reading of the rules as students, not legal advice. A real launch would need a lawyer and the partner bank's compliance team to review it.

Privacy and Data Protection

The main law is the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 dpdp_act. It allows personal data to be processed only for a lawful purpose, based on consent after a clear notice or on certain legitimate uses. The organisation holding the data (the Data Fiduciary) must limit use to that purpose, keep the data accurate, protect it with reasonable security, report breaches to the Data Protection Board and to the affected people, delete data when it is no longer needed, and provide a way to raise grievances. Failing to protect data can be penalised up to ₹250 crore. The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025, and most of the main duties apply about 18 months later, around May 2027 dpdp_rules, so anything we launch would have to comply from the first day.

Until then, Section 43A of the IT Act, 2000 and the SPDI Rules, 2011 still apply to sensitive personal data, which includes financial information itact, spdi. Section 72A covers disclosing personal data in breach of contract. Sections 66C and 66D (identity theft and cheating by personation) describe the very offences we are trying to prevent. The CERT-In directions of April 2022 require reporting cyber incidents within 6 hours and keeping logs for 180 days in India certin. RBI's 2018 circular on payment data storage requires payment data to be stored only in India rbi_local. If the product were ever offered in Europe, the GDPR would also apply, including Article 22 on decisions made only by automated means gdpr.

Our draft privacy policy would say the following. We collect account details, payment details (amount, payee, time), a hashed device ID, login results, answers to the scam questions and reports filed. We do not collect contacts, SMS, call logs, location, card numbers or bank passwords. The data is used only to prevent fraud, run the wallet and meet legal duties, and never for advertising, credit scoring or sale. Features are kept for 12 months for retraining and disputes, and baselines for 30 days. Logs are kept for as long as the law requires, and after that data is deleted or anonymised. Data is shared only with the user's bank or payment app, with law enforcement when required, and (for confirmed scam payees only) with industry fraud registries. Users can see a summary of their data, ask for corrections or deletion, raise a grievance, and see all their warnings and reports on the My Safety page.

Terms of Service and Consent

The terms would make clear that ScamShield gives warnings. It reduces risk, but it cannot catch every scam, and not every flagged payment is a scam. The user can still continue a MEDIUM payment after reading the warning, and a HIGH payment after the pause and re-typing. Only payments to analyst-confirmed scam accounts are fully blocked, and the account owner can appeal through the grievance channel. Answering the questions is voluntary. Reports must be made in good faith, and a report alone never blocks anyone. Consent would be taken through a separate, simple notice, not hidden in the wallet terms, and withdrawing it would be as easy as giving it. Because fraud checks are part of the payment service, we would also explain up front that withdrawing consent may limit payments through the partner app.

Security Policies

In the prototype, passwords are hashed with bcrypt (cost 10). Sessions use a signed JWT in an httpOnly, SameSite cookie rfc7519, and login errors are generic. Every route checks roles on the server. All inputs are validated with zod, request bodies are limited to 100 kB, security headers are set with helmet, CORS is limited to the web app, and Redis rate limits apply to logins (20 per minute per IP) and payment checks (120 per minute per user). The check token is signed and tied to the user, transaction, amount and payee. Device IDs are hashed, and secrets live in an environment file that is not in the repository. Analyst verdicts, rule changes, retraining and simulator runs are written to an audit log.

Before production we would still need TLS everywhere, encryption at rest, a secrets manager and key rotation, hosting in an Indian region, multi-factor login for analysts and admins, two-person approval for rule changes, tamper-proof logs kept for 180 days, a written incident response plan (CERT-In within 6 hours, plus DPDP notifications), an external penetration test, and a check against OWASP ASVS Level 2 owasp_asvs.

Accessibility and Ethics

Many of our likely users are older or new to digital payments. We aimed for WCAG 2.2 AA wcag, in line with the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 rpwd. Risk is never shown only by colour (each band also has an icon and a label), sentences are short, buttons are big, and the screens work at phone width and in dark mode. Screen reader testing and regional languages are still to be done.

On ethics, the biggest worry is false alarms on honest people, so we chose thresholds by looking at measured false alarm rates and LOW payments see nothing. We use no demographic features. Each user is only compared with their own history, though in a real deployment false alarm rates should be checked separately for groups like seniors and new users. The friction is used only to slow down a likely scam and never for any commercial purpose. Every decision has a readable reason, and a human analyst has to confirm before a payee is blocked. We have also tried to be open about the weak points of our results, which are listed in Chapter 11.

Payments Sector Rules

A bank or payment app using ScamShield would have to fit it into several RBI and NPCI requirements. RBI's 2021 Master Direction on Digital Payment Security Controls asks regulated entities for fraud risk controls including transaction monitoring rbi_dpsc, and ScamShield is one such control. RBI's 2024 Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management require early warning signals and reporting rbi_frm, and our alerts and audit log could feed into that. The 2025 Authentication Directions ask for risk-based checks from April 2026 rbi_auth. NPCI's 2025 guidelines limit API calls npci_api, so scoring has to run inside the bank's own systems. RBI's outsourcing rules would apply if a bank used us as a vendor, which means audit rights, data location and business continuity plans. DoT's FRI could be used as an extra signal, subject to its access terms dot_fri.
