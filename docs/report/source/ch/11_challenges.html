Challenges and Limitations

Challenges Faced and How We Solved Them

Development challenges and their resolution
ChallengeWhat happenedHow we solved it
Results that were too goodThe first generator produced PR-AUC ≈ 0.99. Feature importances showed the model had memorised exact scam amounts (scams used a fixed menu such as ₹4,999, while legitimate amounts never ended in 99) and that every scammer account was days old while almost every legitimate payee was years old.We treated this as a bug, not a result. Legitimate and scam amounts now pass through the same price-point logic; legitimate young payees (new shops, friends joining, festival stalls) and older bought scam accounts were added. PR-AUC fell to a believable ≈ 0.95.
Polluted baselinesEach simulated user was hit by many scams, and scam amounts entered the user's baseline, so a normal ₹500 payment later looked extreme.Fraud-labelled payments are excluded from baselines in both the generator and the API, and drained balances are restored after 1–3 days.
Training/serving skewFeatures are computed in Python for training and in TypeScript for serving; tiny differences (rounding, hour boundaries in IST) would silently corrupt the model's inputs.A shared JSON specification, mirrored JavaScript rounding in Python, and 300 committed parity cases that the TypeScript tests must reproduce exactly.
ML latency under loadSingle-row predictions were slow under concurrency because of OpenMP thread start-up, tripping the circuit breaker in the first load test.OMP_NUM_THREADS=1: ML throughput rose from 47 to 154 req/s; the breaker stayed closed. The fallback worked as designed even before the fix.
Double spending and replaysDouble taps, network retries and concurrent confirms could debit twice.Idempotency keys with SET NX; check tokens bound to amount and payee; conditional SQL updates inside one transaction; tested with five concurrent confirms.
Questionnaire abuseA coached victim could answer “no” to everything to make a warning disappear.Answers can only raise the score; the OTP answer is a hard override.
Explaining a tree ensembleSHAP would have added a heavy dependency and latency.Occlusion explanations: one batched prediction of 21 rows, model-agnostic and easy to explain.
Demo realism without disturbing stateRepeated demo runs changed balances and counters, making later runs behave differently.Dedicated simulator personas whose Redis state and balances are reset before each run; seeded one-click demo logins bound to a “registered device”.
TimeThe core system had to be built in a one-week sprint.Phased plan with the riskiest work (data, model, evaluation) first; a fixed definition of done; simple, well-understood components.

Current Limitations

  1. Synthetic data only. All metrics show that the method works on a realistic simulation; they say nothing about real-world fraud rates. Real scam patterns are more varied, and the simulated fraud rate (≈ 2.4 %) is far higher than real UPI traffic, so precision on real traffic would be lower.
  2. No adversary. Simulated scam behaviour is stationary; real scammers adapt to whatever gets blocked.
  3. No real payment integration. There is no connection to NPCI, a bank or real money; the ledger is internal.
  4. The questionnaire depends on honesty. A coached victim may answer “no” to every question; answers can only raise risk, but they cannot catch a lie.
  5. Weak on legitimate first-time payments. New contacts and new merchants are flagged at 20–30 % in small samples — the hardest legitimate cases.
  6. Calibration drift. The model is over-confident in the 30–60 % range on the holdout's festival period.
  7. Overrides not evaluated offline. The confirmed-fraud-payee override and analyst labels are not exercised in offline evaluation, because the synthetic data has no analyst labels.
  8. Single-instance deployment. One API, one worker and one ML process; tested to about 20 req/s on a laptop.
  9. Reports can be gamed with many accounts. Each account can report a payee once, but fake accounts are not prevented; analyst review remains the authority for blocking.
  10. Approximate baselines. Baselines refresh asynchronously, so a burst of payments may be scored against a baseline a few hundred milliseconds old (velocity counters are synchronous).
  11. Not production-certified. No penetration test, PCI/RBI review, HA deployment or key rotation; secrets are demo defaults.
  12. No primary user validation yet. The design is grounded in published research; structured user-acceptance results are pending (Section 10.5).
