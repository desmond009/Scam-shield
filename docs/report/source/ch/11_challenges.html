Challenges and Limitations

Problems We Faced

Results that were too good. The first version of our data generator gave a PR-AUC of about 0.99, which was suspicious. Feature importance showed two problems. Scam amounts came from a short fixed list (₹4,999 and similar) and genuine amounts never ended in 99, so the model had simply memorised amounts. Also, every scam account was a few days old while almost every genuine payee was years old, so "young payee" meant scam. We fixed the generator: genuine and scam amounts now use the same price logic, and we added young genuine payees (new shops, friends joining, festival stalls) and old bought scam accounts. PR-AUC came down to about 0.95, which is much more believable.

Polluted baselines. Each simulated user was being hit by many scams, and those scam amounts became part of their "normal" history, so later an ordinary ₹500 payment looked extreme. We now leave fraud-labelled payments out of baselines in both the generator and the API, and give drained balances back after a few days.

Python and TypeScript giving different answers. Features are computed in Python for training and TypeScript for serving. Small differences in rounding or in how IST hours are worked out would quietly feed the model wrong inputs. We moved the definitions into one JSON spec, copied JavaScript's rounding into the Python code, and added the 300-case parity test.

Slow ML under load. This was the thread pool issue described in Section 10.4, fixed with one environment variable. It was good to see that the fallback to rules worked as designed even before the fix.

Double taps and retries. A double tap or a network retry could have charged twice. Idempotency keys, a token tied to amount and payee, and conditional SQL updates in one transaction fixed this, and we tested it with five confirms at the same time.

Repeatable demos. Every demo run changed balances and counters, so the next run behaved differently. We gave the simulator its own set of accounts and reset their state before each run.

Current Limitations

  • Everything is tested on synthetic data. The numbers show that the method works on a realistic simulation, not how it would do on real UPI traffic. The fraud rate in our data (about 2.4 %) is much higher than real life, so real precision would be lower.
  • Our simulated scammers do not adapt. Real ones change tactics when they get blocked.
  • There is no connection to NPCI, a bank or real money.
  • The questions depend on honesty. A coached victim can say "no" to everything. The score will not go down, but the lie is not caught either.
  • First payments to new contacts and new merchants are flagged too often (20 to 30 % in small samples).
  • The model is over-confident in the 30 to 60 % range during the festival period.
  • The confirmed-scam override and analyst labels are not tested offline, since the synthetic data has no analyst labels.
  • Everything runs as one instance and was only tested to about 20 requests a second on a laptop.
  • Someone could create many accounts to report a payee. Reports never block on their own, but this is not prevented.
  • Baselines update in the background, so in a quick burst of payments a few may be scored against a slightly old baseline.
  • No penetration test or audit has been done, and the secrets are demo defaults.
  • We have not yet run proper user testing (Section 10.5).
