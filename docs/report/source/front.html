Abstract

The Unified Payments Interface (UPI) processed more than 24,000 crore transactions in FY2025-26, and its speed and irreversibility have made it a preferred channel for fraud. Most UPI fraud is not a technical break-in: the victim is persuaded — by a fake bank or KYC agent, a fake police officer, a “job offer” or a remote-access app — to authorise the payment personally. Once the money has moved, recovery is slow and uncertain. Existing defences are largely after the fact (complaint helplines, account freezing) or operate inside banks without explaining anything to the payer at the moment of decision.

This project presents ScamShield, a pre-transaction scam-detection and intervention system for a UPI-style wallet. Every payment is scored before it is authorised. A feature builder computes 20 behavioural features from Redis and PostgreSQL (amount relative to the payer's 30-day baseline, payee age, distinct senders to the payee in 24 hours, velocity, new device, failed attempts and others). Eleven configurable rules and a calibrated gradient-boosting model (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting with isotonic calibration) produce two scores that are blended into a 0–100 risk score with LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH bands and plain-language reasons. The application intervenes proportionately: nothing for LOW; a warning with three “scam-context” questions for MEDIUM; and a blocking screen with a ten-second pause and mandatory re-typing of the recipient's UPI ID for HIGH. Analyst-confirmed scam accounts are blocked for all users. Analysts review alerts live over Server-Sent Events, and their labels feed back into payee reputation and model retraining.

The system is built as a containerised monorepo (Next.js, Express, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis, BullMQ). Because real fraud data is not available to students, we built a seeded synthetic data generator with four user personas, realistic “hard negatives” and four scam scenarios, and evaluated on a time-based holdout of 45,387 payments (1.72 % scams). At the false-positive rate of the rules-only baseline (3.7 %), the hybrid engine catches 95.6 % of scams versus 73.3 % for rules alone; at the MEDIUM threshold it achieves 79.1 % precision and 93.2 % recall at a 0.43 % false-positive rate. We report honestly that the ML-only model ranks payments slightly better (PR-AUC 0.946 versus 0.933), and that two data leaks discovered during development were fixed rather than reported as gains. The 95th-percentile latency of the scoring endpoint was about 110 ms at 20 requests per second, against a 300 ms budget; 341 automated tests pass. The report also documents the product case — user research, market analysis, legal compliance under the DPDP Act 2023 and RBI directions, and a launch plan — while being explicit that the results come from synthetic data and do not represent real-world fraud rates.

Keywords: UPI, authorised push payment fraud, scam detection, gradient boosting, probability calibration, explainable risk scoring, security warnings, DPDP Act.
