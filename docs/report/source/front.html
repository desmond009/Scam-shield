Abstract

UPI handled more than 24,000 crore transactions in FY2025-26, and fraud on it has grown with it. Most of this fraud is not hacking. The victim is talked into making the payment themselves by a fake bank or KYC agent, a fake police officer, a job offer or a remote-access app. PINs and OTPs do not help here, and once the money is sent it is hard to get back.

In this project we built ScamShield, a system that checks a UPI-style payment for scam risk before the money moves. For each payment it computes 20 behavioural features, such as how the amount compares with the user's last 30 days, how old the receiving account is, and how many different people paid that account today. Eleven rules and a calibrated gradient boosting model each give a score, and the two are blended into a 0 to 100 risk score with a LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH band and reasons in plain language. LOW payments go through with no interruption. MEDIUM payments show a warning and three short questions about the situation, such as whether someone is on the phone asking them to pay. HIGH payments get a 10-second pause and the payer has to type the UPI ID again. Analysts see alerts live, and when they confirm a scam account it is blocked for all users.

The system is built with Next.js, Express, FastAPI, PostgreSQL and Redis, and runs with Docker. Since real fraud data was not available, we wrote a synthetic data generator with realistic genuine and scam behaviour and tested on a later time period of 45,387 payments. At the same false alarm rate as a rules-only system, the hybrid caught 95.6 % of scams against 73.3 %. ML alone ranked payments slightly better than the hybrid (PR-AUC 0.946 against 0.933), and the report discusses this trade-off. The 95th percentile response time was about 110 ms, and all 341 automated tests pass. All results are on synthetic data, and testing on real data is the main next step.

Keywords: UPI, scam detection, authorised push payment fraud, gradient boosting, calibration, security warnings.
